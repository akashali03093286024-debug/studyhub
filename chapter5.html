Chapter 5: pH & Buffers

Pharm D • Physical Pharmacy

1. What is pH?

pH is a measure of the acidity or basicity of an aqueous solution.

Easy Definition:

pH tells us whether a solution is acidic, neutral or basic.

Formula: pH = −log[H⁺]

2. pH Scale

pH less than 7: Acidic

pH equal to 7: Neutral

pH greater than 7: Basic / Alkaline

Generally, a lower pH means a higher hydrogen ion concentration, while a higher pH means a lower hydrogen ion concentration.

3. What is a Buffer?

A buffer is a solution that resists a significant change in pH when small amounts of acid or base are added.

Easy Definition:

A buffer helps keep the pH relatively stable.

4. Types of Buffers

Acidic Buffer

Usually consists of a weak acid and its conjugate base, often supplied by a salt of that weak acid.

Basic Buffer

Usually consists of a weak base and its conjugate acid, often supplied by a salt of that weak base.

5. Pharmaceutical Importance

  • Buffers help maintain the desired pH of pharmaceutical formulations.
  • They can improve the stability of some drugs.
  • They can help maintain suitable pH for preparations.
  • pH can affect drug solubility and ionization.
  • Buffers are important in many pharmaceutical and biological systems.
