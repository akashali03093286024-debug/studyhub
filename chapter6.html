Chapter 6: Electrolytes

Physical Pharmacy — Notes + Interactive MCQs

What are Electrolytes?

Easy Definition:
Electrolytes are substances that produce ions when dissolved in water and allow the solution to conduct electricity.

Examples include sodium chloride (NaCl), hydrochloric acid (HCl), potassium chloride (KCl), and other ionic or ionizable substances.

Strong Electrolytes

Strong electrolytes dissociate or ionize almost completely in solution.

  • HCl
  • NaCl
  • KCl
  • Strong acids and many soluble salts
Remember: Strong electrolyte → almost complete ion formation.

Weak Electrolytes

Weak electrolytes ionize only partially in solution.

  • Acetic acid (CH₃COOH)
  • Ammonia solution
  • Other weak acids and weak bases
Remember: Weak electrolyte → partial ion formation.

Cations and Anions

Cation: A positively charged ion.

Examples: Na⁺, K⁺, Ca²⁺

Anion: A negatively charged ion.

Examples: Cl⁻, OH⁻

Memory Trick:
Cation = Positive (+)
Anion = Negative (−)

Dissociation

Dissociation is the separation of an ionic compound into ions that were already present in the compound.

NaCl → Na⁺ + Cl⁻

Ionization

Ionization is the formation of ions from a molecular substance when it reacts with or dissolves in water.

For example, a weak molecular acid can form ions in water.

Simple Difference:
Dissociation → existing ions separate.
Ionization → ions are formed.

Pharmaceutical Importance

  • Electrolytes help maintain body-fluid balance.
  • They are important for nerve function.
  • They help in normal muscle function.
  • Electrolytes are used in oral rehydration solutions (ORS).
  • They help maintain osmotic balance.
  • They are important in pharmaceutical formulations.
